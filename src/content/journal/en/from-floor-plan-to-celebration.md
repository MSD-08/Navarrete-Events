Photographs of a celebration show the result. They rarely show the floor plan, the spreadsheet or the early hours of the build. Yet almost everything that makes an evening feel effortless is decided there.

## The floor plan as the first design

For the 2024 graduating class of the Preparatoria Federal Lázaro Cárdenas, the starting point was a floor plan: entrances, emergency exits, planted areas, dance floor, DJ booth and the position of every table, with measured distances between them. The aerial view on the night is, in essence, that same drawing made real.

![The event floor plan](media:_reveal/pflc-before) ![An aerial view of the finished event](media:_reveal/pflc-after "PFLC 2024 graduation — from floor plan to aerial view")

A good floor plan doesn’t just seat people; it anticipates movement. Where the bar queue forms, where the waiting staff come through, what guests see as they arrive and which tables have the best view of the dance floor.

## The blank canvas

For Michelle’s quinceañera, the starting point was an open, contemporary space with columns and artificial turf. The design turned it into a hacienda: terracotta walls, arches dressed in greenery, clay vessels and warm light. Between one image and the other lie weeks of design, production and coordination.

![The space before the build](media:xv-michelle/before) ![The finished space](media:xv-michelle/after "Michelle’s quinceañera — before and after")

## The build is part of the design

A large-scale set, such as the neon city of Allisson’s quinceañera, is also designed around how it will be built: what arrives first, what is assembled on site, how long each stage takes and what must be ready before the first light goes on.
