A centrepiece is looked at. An installation is moved through. The difference is not one of size but of intention: at a certain scale, flowers stop accompanying a space and begin to define it.

## A stroke in the air

At J & C’s wedding, the head table was resolved with an installation of white orchids rising like a calligraphic stroke. Against black and between crystal candelabra, the form reads from anywhere in the room and serves as a backdrop for the evening’s most important moments.

![An installation of white orchids with candelabra](media:boda-jc/g21 "J & C wedding — orchid installation")

## Columns, not arrangements

At E & K’s wedding, flowers were built into a structure: columns of white rods wrapped in foliage, opening at the top like a goblet. The chromatic discipline — white, green and light — lets the form take the lead.

![A sculptural column wrapped in foliage and white flowers](media:boda-ek/g11) ![White roses and hydrangeas on the table](media:boda-ek/g13 "E & K wedding — structure and table")

## A space made of flowers

Camila’s quinceañera took the idea further: tunnels covered entirely in roses leading to the dance floor, concentric arches around the stage and a suspended floral cloud crossing the room. Here flowers don’t decorate a space — they build it.

![A tunnel covered in roses](media:xv-camila/g09) ![Concentric floral arches](media:xv-camila/g02 "Camila’s quinceañera — tunnels and arches")

## What lies behind

An installation of this scale begins on paper: measurements, structure, rigging points, lighting and build schedules. The flowers are the final layer of work that is, above all, ephemeral architecture.
