There is a question worth asking before choosing a single flower: what will this place look like at ten o’clock at night? The answer is rarely in the colour palette. It is almost always in the light.

Light is the most flexible material we work with. It can make a large room feel intimate, turn a long table into a path, transform a dance floor into a stage. And unlike almost everything else, it changes as the evening unfolds.

![An imperial table with candles, white tulips and crystal](media:boda-ta/g01 "T & A wedding — candelabra and tulips on the imperial table")

## The candle as a unit of measure

On a table, the candle sets the human scale. Its glow cannot light a room — which is exactly why it matters: it gathers attention to the distance of a conversation. Mixing heights, low votives with tall tapers in candelabra, creates depth without adding objects.

At T & A’s wedding the whole room followed that logic: a draped ceiling that lowers the perceived height, a single chandelier at the centre, and hundreds of points of warm light along the imperial tables.

![Lit tapers in crystal candelabra](media:boda-jc/g23) ![Pillar candles on a wooden ledge](media:boda-dm/g13 "Candles at the J & C and D & M weddings")

## The chandelier as architecture

A chandelier is not an accessory; it is a point of order. It defines the centre of a space and gives the ceiling a presence that would otherwise disappear into darkness. At Montse’s quinceañera, monumental chandeliers hung from a curtain of crystals falling like rain; as stage lighting passed through it, the entire ceiling seemed to move.

![A crystal chandelier above tables of white flowers](media:xv-montse/g07 "Montse’s quinceañera — chandelier and crystal rain")

## Shadow is designed too

What remains in shadow matters as much as what is lit. Shadow hides what adds nothing — structures, cables, featureless walls — and gives the eye a place to rest between one point of light and the next. A fully lit room rarely feels luxurious.

That is why we think about lighting at the beginning of the design, not the end: it decides which materials shine, which colours survive the night and which moments deserve a change of atmosphere.
